# Question 2

## Problem
The Computer Anxiety Rating Scale (CARS) measures an individual's level of computer anxiety on a scale from 20 (no anxiety) to 100 (highest level of anxiety). Researchers at Miami University administered CARS to 172 business students. One of the objectives of the study was to determine if there is a difference between the level of computer anxiety experienced by female and male business students. We assume that variances of the two populations are equal.

| | Males | Females |
|---|---|---|
| $\bar{x}$ | 40.26 | 36.85 |
| $s$ | 13.35 | 9.42 |
| $n$ | 100 | 72 |

At the 0.05 level of significance, is there evidence of a difference in the mean computer anxiety experienced by female and male business students?

## Solution

**Given**

$$n_1 = 100, \quad \bar{X}_1 = 40.26, \quad S_1 = 13.35$$
$$n_2 = 72, \quad \bar{X}_2 = 36.85, \quad S_2 = 9.42$$

**1. Hypotheses**

$$H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 \quad \text{versus} \quad H_a: \mu_1 \ne \mu_2$$

**2. Significance level**

$$\alpha = 0.05$$

**3. Test type**

Since $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ are unknown and assumed equal, this is a pooled-variance *t* test, with $df = n_1 + n_2 - 2 = 170$.

**4. Critical values**

For $\alpha = 0.05$ (two-tailed), the critical *t* values are $\pm 1.96$.

**5. Test statistic**

Pooled variance:

$$S_p^2 = \frac{(n_1 - 1)S_1^2 + (n_2 - 1)S_2^2}{(n_1 - 1) + (n_2 - 1)} = \frac{(100-1)(13.35)^2 + (72-1)(9.42)^2}{(100-1) + (72-1)} = 140.85$$

Test statistic:

$$t = \frac{(\bar{X}_1 - \bar{X}_2) - (\mu_1 - \mu_2)}{\sqrt{S_p^2\left(\frac{1}{n_1} + \frac{1}{n_2}\right)}} = \frac{(40.26 - 36.85) - 0}{\sqrt{140.85\left(\frac{1}{100} + \frac{1}{72}\right)}} = 1.859$$

**6. Decision and conclusion**

Since $t = 1.859 < 1.96$, do not reject $H_0$.

There is no sufficient evidence of a difference in the mean computer anxiety experienced by female and male business students.
